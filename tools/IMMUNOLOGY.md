# Immunology series (owner-only section)

A new hub section, **Immunology**, for the owner's own learning. It is opened only by the owner password:
no course code in `tools/access.json` lists these primers, and none should until the owner says so.

## Brief from the owner

* Depth: start at graduate-textbook level (Janeway, Abbas) and go to research depth, citing the key primary
  papers on the plates.
* Species: mouse and human, then compare and contrast. Say which species each result comes from, and give a
  short "mouse vs human" note wherever they differ.
* "The paradigm": the field's paradigms (clonal selection, self/non-self and tolerance, MHC restriction, the
  two-signal model, pattern recognition, the danger model), each told through its decisive experiment.
* Order: foundations first, then the owner's focus topics.
* Focus topics, with extra detail: how IgA is produced; how antibodies against carbohydrates develop
  (including conjugate vaccines); gut immunity; oral and food-based vaccines.

## Plan (one primer at a time; each is vetted before the next starts)

Part I, Foundations
1. Innate instructs adaptive: pattern recognition, dendritic-cell maturation and migration, signal 1, 2, 3.
2. Antigen receptors and their genes: antibody and TCR structure, V(D)J recombination, allelic exclusion,
   BCR and TCR signalling.
3. Antigen presentation: MHC class I and II pathways, cross-presentation, CD1 and MR1.
4. Lymphocyte development and tolerance: B cells in bone marrow (editing, anergy, B-1, marginal zone,
   follicular); T cells in the thymus (positive and negative selection, AIRE, thymic Tregs).
5. T helper subsets: Th1, Th2, Th17, Tfh, Treg, their transcription factors and cytokine loops.

Part II, Making antibodies
6. The germinal centre: Tfh help, zones, AID, somatic hypermutation, selection, plasma vs memory fate.
7. Class switching and effector functions: switch regions, cytokine control, Fc receptors, complement,
   FcRn, IgG subclasses (and how they differ between mouse and human).
8. Memory and vaccine principles: recall responses, adjuvants, correlates of protection, prime–boost.

Part III, Focus topics
9. How IgA is made: T-dependent (Peyer's patches, M cells, Tfh, TGF-β and retinoic acid) and
   T-independent (isolated lymphoid follicles, lamina propria, APRIL and BAFF) switching; gut homing;
   J chain, pIgR transcytosis and secretory component; IgA1 and IgA2.
10. What IgA does: immune exclusion, enchained growth, microbiota coating, polyreactive vs high-affinity
    IgA, IgA deficiency, breast-milk SIgA.
11. Antibodies to carbohydrates: TI-1 and TI-2 antigens, marginal-zone and B-1 cells, the response in
    infants, natural antibodies, how antibodies bind glycans, polysaccharide vs conjugate vaccines and
    the origin of T-cell help; links to the Glycans primer.
12. Gut immunity: mucus and epithelial barrier, gut lymphoid tissue, oral tolerance, microbiota shaping of
    Tregs, Th17 and ILC3s, the IgA–microbiota loop.
13. Oral and food-based vaccines: why oral delivery is hard, licensed oral vaccines as case studies,
    plant-made and edible vaccines (history and current status), measuring mucosal protection.

Part IV, Ideas
14. The paradigms of immunology, each through its experiment.
